Why this pledge exists A different way to survive

The Pledge.

In a world where we have monetized survival, mass production, and AI, should we be asking why representative currency gets to dictate our morals and work-life balance? When the job market is drastically insufficient to support the cost of living countrywide, shouldn’t we wonder whether there is a better way? A moneyless society won’t fix every problem. But if it makes the world better, or even just solves some of our problems, isn’t it worth giving it a shot?

I take the pledge to try a different kind of system. To cut the corporate fuel that drives cortisol spikes in every home. To cooperate and build a sense of social responsibility so we can ALL thrive. To turn back to the village and get in touch with my human nature.

Five core commitments

What signing means

  1. 1

    I Reject Currency as a Measure of Human Worth

    A person’s value is inherent, not earned through labor or wealth. Whether a neighbor is able-bodied or paralyzed, rich in skills or in need of food and shelter, they deserve dignity, safety, and care. I practice Ubuntu—“I am because we are”—knowing my well-being is bound to my community’s.

  2. 2

    I Will Give Freely Without Keeping Score

    I enter this space to build mutual self-reliance, not to extract profit. I will not keep a ledger of owed favors or hoard what the community can share.

    • If I can work or create: I offer real, practical skills, labor, or mentorship freely, with no expectation of direct repayment.
    • If I provide goods: I offer them freely, with no price tag, deposit, or swap requirement.
    • If a project needs materials: I ask the person requesting the work to cover their exact receipt price when the materials cannot be found free on Unbought. I add no markup and take no profit, and the worker never has to front the cost.
    • If I cannot work: due to age, disability, illness, or capacity, I accept care without shame, knowing my presence and humanity are enough.
  3. 3

    I Honor Consent and Personal Boundaries

    This is a free, non-hierarchical society built on voluntary cooperation, not coercion. I respect bodily autonomy, time, energy, and boundaries. “No” is a complete sentence, and every exchange of labor, housing, or mentorship requires clear, ongoing mutual consent.

  4. 4

    I Commit to Lifelong Learning and Collective Empowerment

    Education is a birthright, not a commodity. If I hold knowledge or a trade, I share it openly. If I am learning, I approach mentorship with humility and dedication so I can pass those skills forward and help free my community from dependence on the market.

  5. 5

    I Stand Against Exploitation and Gatekeeping

    I will not use Unbought's commons, labor, goods, or land-trust resources to fuel outside businesses, hoard wealth, or exploit others. Members access the commons through mutual aid; non-member purchases support the Unbought donation fund. I hold myself and fellow members accountable through restorative dialogue, transparency, and shared social responsibility.

What taking the pledge means

Take the Pledge to join the commons.

Taking the pledge is a commitment to show up as a neighbor, not a customer: offer what you can, ask for what you need without shame, respect every boundary, and help keep this commons free from profit and gatekeeping. You can browse labor and goods without an account; posting offers, requesting help, messaging, and managing a track require membership. Your pledge and private details stay in this browser. Public listings and community posts are shared through Supabase when connected, and you can withdraw at any time.

Phase 1 minimum: joining asks for a pledge of at least 1 hour per week. We cannot offer housing yet, so the ask is intentionally small. Members who cannot work are still welcome in the Care Circle with no labor obligation.
