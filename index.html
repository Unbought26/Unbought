Why we exist

We are opting out of the marketplace—not out of each other.

Unbought is a neighbor-built society where survival does not require a price tag. We practice the economy we actually need: one where labor is a gift, goods circulate at cost, education is a commons, and land is held in trust for the people who live on it.

We are not anti-work. We are anti-extraction. We believe paid-level skill should be shared without a paywall, that materials should never carry hidden profit, and that every person—working or not—deserves care, belonging, and a place to call home.

Ubuntu

“I am because we are.”

Every person has inherent worth, whether or not they can work, produce, or pay. Care is not a reward for productivity. It is the ground we stand on.

1972 Origin story

Why “Unbought”?

“I am unbought and unbossed.”

— Shirley Chisholm, U.S. House of Representatives, 1972

Shirley Chisholm’s rallying cry was a refusal: no donor, no boss, no price could own her voice. We carry that spirit into everyday life. Our labor, our learning, our homes, and our care should remain outside the reach of currency and corruption.

Unbought is not a brand to buy into. It is a practice to build.

Five core commitments

What signing means

  1. 1

    I Reject Currency as a Measure of Human Worth

    A person’s value is inherent, not earned through labor or wealth. Whether a neighbor is able-bodied or paralyzed, rich in skills or in need of food and shelter, they deserve dignity, safety, and care. I practice Ubuntu—“I am because we are”—knowing my well-being is bound to my community’s.

  2. 2

    I Will Give Freely Without Keeping Score

    I enter this space to build mutual self-reliance, not to extract profit. I will not keep a ledger of owed favors or hoard what the community can share.

    • If I can work or create: I offer real, practical skills, labor, or mentorship freely, with no expectation of direct repayment.
    • If I provide goods or materials: I offer them free or strictly at cost—the exact receipt price—with no markup or profit.
    • If I cannot work due to age, disability, illness, or capacity: I accept care without shame, knowing my presence and humanity are enough.
  3. 3

    I Honor Consent and Personal Boundaries

    This is a free, non-hierarchical society built on voluntary cooperation, not coercion. I respect bodily autonomy, time, energy, and boundaries. “No” is a complete sentence, and every exchange of labor, housing, or mentorship requires clear, ongoing mutual consent.

  4. 4

    I Commit to Lifelong Learning and Collective Empowerment

    Education is a birthright, not a commodity. If I hold knowledge or a trade, I share it openly. If I am learning, I approach mentorship with humility and dedication so I can pass those skills forward and help free my community from dependence on the market.

  5. 5

    I Stand Against Exploitation and Gatekeeping

    I will not use Unbought’s free labor, free goods, or land-trust resources to fuel outside businesses, hoard wealth, or exploit others. I hold myself and fellow members accountable through restorative dialogue, transparency, and shared social responsibility.

Safety & scope guardrails

Unbought Academy

Learn from a neighbor. Build for the commons.

Professionals and tradespeople teach apprentices online or through local guilds. No tuition, no debt, no gatekeeping—just real skills practiced on real community projects.

“The best way to learn a trade is beside someone who trusts you with the work.”

An apprentice joins a guild, learns safety and craft, and contributes to a project the community actually needs—a ramp, a garden bed, a nonprofit website, a repair café. At the end, peers who saw the work verify the skill.

  1. 1
    LearnMentor-led practice, online or local
  2. 2
    BuildComplete a real community project
  3. 3
    ReflectReview scope, safety, and consent
  4. 4
    VerifyTwo peers sign off on the skill

Badges are community-verified, not accredited credentials. Regulated work remains within the mentor’s scope of practice and local law.

Mentoring counts. Teaching and mentoring hours fulfill an Active Contributor’s monthly service pledge—direct tasks and skill-sharing carry the same weight.

Open curriculum. Peer-verified projects build toward an open-source curriculum library, so learning stays free, credible, and available to the next neighbor.

Care Circle governance

Care is governed by consent, not suspicion.

A preview of how peer care circles make decisions. No medical diagnosis is requested or stored. No one is scored for productivity. Safety and dignity move together.

Step one

Ubuntu reciprocal reflection

Every care connection begins with questions asked in both directions. The person offering support reflects too—care is a relationship, not a transaction.

No diagnosis requested

“This isn’t a job interview or an interrogation. In a capitalist system, we are trained to prove our worth through productivity and hide our needs out of shame. Today, we are practicing Ubuntu—the truth that my well-being is tied to yours. I’m going to ask you a few questions, and I’ll share my own answers too, so we start as equals.”

01

What would support feel like this season?

Practical help, company, quiet, a ride, a meal—or simply being believed. The answer belongs to the person receiving care.

02

Normalizing need

Think of a time when you couldn’t do something on your own and someone stepped in to help. How did it feel to receive that help, and what was hard about accepting it?

03

Redefining human worth

If you were completely unable to work, produce, or do favors for anyone for the next year, what do you believe your value to a community would still be?

04

Honest capacity

In a world where nobody is forcing you to clock in, what kind of contribution actually feels energizing and sustainable right now—and what drains you or harms your health?

05

Solidarity without scorekeeping

In Unbought, some members build houses or fix cars while others are chronically ill, elderly, or in crisis and cannot work. How do you feel about giving your time knowing you might never get a direct favor back from that specific person?

06

Consent & boundaries

Imagine a neighbor asks for help with a project, and halfway through you realize you don’t have the time, tools, or energy to finish. How would you communicate that boundary with care?

07

Restorative accountability

If you felt another member was acting out of an “every person for themselves” mindset, how would you want the community to approach them—and how would you want to be approached if it were you?

Step two

Blind two-reviewer anti-bias check

When a care request needs a circle decision, identifying details are removed. Two reviewers assess the same criteria independently before they compare notes.

Two-reviewer rule
Independent review rubric used before a care request is matched
Review criterion Reviewer A Reviewer B Shared agreement
Consent is clear and ongoing Met Met Both agree
Request is within scope of practice Met Met Both agree
No extractive or coercive intent Met Met Both agree
Accommodations and access needs considered Met Needs more conversation Discuss, do not deny

Identifiers removed: names, addresses, diagnoses, immigration status, and income are not shared with reviewers.

No algorithm decides: reviewers must both agree to proceed. When they disagree, the circle returns to the person with a question—not a rejection.

Step three

The 90-day reflection pause

If a request shows signs of extractive or capitalistic intent—selling free care, recruiting unpaid labor for a private venture, or using aid as leverage—the circle pauses rather than punishes.

Not a permanent record
  1. 1

    A concern is raised

    Any participant can ask for a pause without needing to prove bad intent. The request is paused immediately.

  2. 2

    Support and clarity are offered

    The person receives a plain-language explanation, a chance to respond, and access to an advocate if they want one.

  3. 3

    Ninety days of reflection

    No public ledger, no permanent ban. The person is invited to reflect on power, consent, and the difference between need and extraction.

  4. 4

    Return with mutual agreement

    After 90 days, the circle reconvenes. The request may resume with new boundaries, a different form of support, or a respectful no.

Ubuntu does not mean endless access. It means we stay in relationship while we protect the community. A boundary can be an act of care.

Phase 2 Housing & land trust

Land should belong to the people who tend it.

We are organizing toward community land trusts: land held permanently for shared use, homes kept affordable across generations, and decisions made by the people who live there—not by distant owners seeking a return.

Legal status: “Unbought Community Trust” is a working name for a structure still in formation. We are seeking pro-bono cooperative legal support or a fiscal sponsor before accepting property deeds. No deed is accepted until the trust structure, stewardship agreement, and resident governance are in place. Nothing here is an offer of housing, credit, or a financial product, and the trust is not yet a registered land trust or a tax-deductible charity.

Homes in trust. Power shared.

For future residents

Tell us what a safe, accessible home would make possible for you. The inquiry asks about household size and access needs—not credit scores or productivity.

For landholders

Explore donation, a long-term community lease, or a below-market sale with the land circle. No pressure, no hidden agenda—just a conversation about stewardship.

Governance by neighbors

Residents and community members hold the majority voice in trust decisions. Affordability is protected by a resale formula, not left to the speculative market.

Accessibility statement

A commons that leaves people out is not a commons.

Unbought intends to meet WCAG 2.2 AA across this site. Keyboard access, visible focus, sufficient contrast, captions, reduced-motion support, and plain-language structure are part of the build—not an afterthought.

This prototype uses semantic HTML, native dialogs, ARIA live announcements, and no third-party tracking or ad code. Accessibility is a practice, not a one-time audit: the community will keep testing with disabled neighbors and publish what changes.

Known prototype limits

  • The pledge film is a placeholder with English captions; a fully described and translated production is still to come.
  • No live support inbox, assisted sign-up line, or moderation queue exists yet.
  • Listings, signatures, and inquiries are stored only in this browser, so there is no shared service or account recovery.

Reporting a barrier

Before launch, Unbought will publish a named accessibility steward and a direct reporting channel. This prototype has no outbound support inbox, so the commitment is documented here rather than implied.

If a page, form, or interaction blocks you, that is a defect to fix—not a failure on your part.
