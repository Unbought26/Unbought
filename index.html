Why we exist

We are opting out of the marketplace—not out of each other.

Unbought is a neighbor-built society where survival does not require a price tag. We practice the economy we actually need: one where labor is a gift, goods circulate freely, education is a commons, and land is held in trust for the people who live on it.

We are not anti-work. We are anti-extraction. We believe paid-level skill should be shared without a paywall, that materials should never carry hidden profit, and that every person—working or not—deserves care, belonging, and a place to call home.

Ubuntu

“I am because we are.”

Every person has inherent worth, whether or not they can work, produce, or pay. Care is not a reward for productivity. It is the ground we stand on.

1972 Origin story

Why “Unbought”?

“I am unbought and unbossed.”

— Shirley Chisholm, U.S. House of Representatives, 1972

Shirley Chisholm’s rallying cry was a refusal: no donor, no boss, no price could own her voice. We carry that spirit into everyday life. Our labor, our learning, our homes, and our care should remain outside the reach of currency and corruption.

Unbought is not a brand to buy into. It is a practice to build.

In progress 01

Free Labor & Requester-Covered Materials

Neighbors offer the same quality of work they would be paid for—repairs, care, tutoring, design, legal literacy, and more—without charging for their time. Labor is free for the worker and the person requesting it. If a project needs materials that cannot be found free on Unbought, the person requesting the work pays their exact receipt price. No markup, no hidden fee.

  • Labor free for worker and requester
  • Requester covers materials only if not found free
  • Receipt shown, zero markup

Why Unbought exists

A moneyless society, for the people, by the people.

A note from the founder

  • Make survival possible without a price tag.
  • Move resources through consent, not purchase.
  • Build the option, then choose it.

Capitalism has been using currency to line the pockets of the few by exploiting the many.

People take advantage of the current system at the cost of human life, hoarding resources and wealth. The people who hold billions are so far removed from the people who made them that the harm disappears into a balance sheet. I kept asking: how do you stop that from happening?

“If you remove the fuel that gives them power, they cease to be relevant.”

That is the wager behind Unbought. Unbought isn’t the next Amazon. It’s an option—an option that opens up opportunities. Maybe you start part time and get a few things done with free labor. Maybe there is land, and you end up doing it full time with shared access to tools, food, and support.

It’s the option to opt out of the capitalistic structures we allow to enslave us. A chance to level the playing field and actually do something about the handful of major issues that come with subscribing to an economy.

We tried feudalism and it didn’t work. We tried monarchy, dictatorship, and empire, and they caused more problems than they were worth. Now it’s time to try a moneyless society, for the people, by the people. Actual freedom. A new way to live and access survival. A new way to thrive.

— A note from the founder of Unbought

Membership

Signing the pledge is how you join.

The pledge is our membership agreement. Read the five commitments, then complete the sign-up form with your details, a username, and a password to open your dashboard. Your membership details stay in this browser—not on a server, not for sale.

The five commitments, in brief

  1. Reject currency as a measure of human worth. Every person has inherent dignity, whether or not they can work.
  2. Give freely without keeping score. Labor and goods are shared; only exact material costs may change hands.
  3. Honor consent and personal boundaries. Every exchange requires clear, ongoing mutual agreement.
  4. Commit to lifelong learning. Skills are taught openly and passed forward.
  5. Stand against exploitation and gatekeeping. The commons serve mutual aid, never outside profit.

How joining works

Browse freely. Participate as a member.

Anyone can browse labor, goods, and this page. Posting, requesting, messaging, and your dashboard require the pledge. Phase 1 asks for at least 1 hour a week because housing is not available yet; the Care Circle has no labor obligation for people who cannot work.

Accessibility statement

A commons that leaves people out is not a commons.

Unbought intends to meet WCAG 2.2 AA across this site. Keyboard access, visible focus, sufficient contrast, captions, reduced-motion support, and plain-language structure are part of the build—not an afterthought.

This prototype uses semantic HTML, native dialogs, ARIA live announcements, and no third-party tracking or ad code. Accessibility is a practice, not a one-time audit: the community will keep testing with disabled neighbors and publish what changes.

Known prototype limits

  • The pledge film is a placeholder with English captions; a fully described and translated production is still to come.
  • No live support inbox, assisted sign-up line, or moderation queue exists yet.
  • Listings, membership records, and waiting-list entries are stored only in this browser, so there is no shared service or account recovery.

Reporting a barrier

Before launch, Unbought will publish a named accessibility steward and a direct reporting channel. This prototype has no outbound support inbox, so the commitment is documented here rather than implied.

If a page, form, or interaction blocks you, that is a defect to fix—not a failure on your part.
