Fund priority

A published order, not invented percentages.

Every gift follows the same order. If a higher priority is already covered, the next one receives the support. The trust will publish a plain-language ledger so neighbors can see what moved and why.

  1. 1

    Legal & trust formation

    Cooperative counsel, fiscal sponsorship, governance documents, filings, and the agreements that keep land and money accountable to residents.

    First priority
  2. 2

    Website, insurance, accounting & operations

    Hosting and domain costs, liability insurance, bookkeeping, accessibility audits, and the unglamorous work of staying reachable and solvent.

    Keep the lights on
  3. 3

    Real estate & land

    Due diligence, stewardship planning, accessibility repairs, and land or buildings held permanently for community use.

    Land & housing
  4. 4

    Free Goods Fund

    Any excess buys products neighbors can claim at no cost on the Free Stuff page—tools, food, clothing, furniture, seeds, and household supplies.

    Excess only

The Free Goods Fund is the overflow valve. It never competes with legal formation, operations, or land stewardship; it receives only what remains after those priorities are covered.

Donating a home? You keep full lifetime access. A house given to the commons stays your home—you can live there until you choose to move or until you die. Pledge 30+ hours a week and Unbought takes over the taxes, or pay the taxes yourself and stay part-time (1–15 hours). Read how home donations work.

Allocation plan

How a dollar moves through the fund.

These are planned stages, not money already held. They do not change the published priority order, and the Free Goods Fund stays donation priority #4.

  1. Stage 1

    Before rollout

    Every dollar that goes into the Unbought donation fund starts with getting Unbought off the ground: licensing, web hosting, database setup, and social media awareness. During beta the practical plan is to get the database, mailer, and social channels rolling for free first. Donations stay closed while we open a dedicated fund account and leave it unused until a lawyer and accountant are on board. No pledge service or payment link will be published before then. This is the pre-rollout operation goal before the March 20, 2027 rollout. No fund account is connected, and no money is charged or transmitted in this prototype.

  2. Stage 2

    Land Trust account

    Once the Land Trust account is formed and the initial start-up costs are paid, the fund funnels into an account while we are seeking accountants. It builds there until we can buy our first plot of land, house, or warehouse. The first house will be in the place where the most Unbought members live. We also have to think about how to scale: local Groups keep scouting their own areas, while the first purchase follows the densest membership. This is a planning rule, not a purchase or an offer, and nothing is transmitted.

  3. Stage 3

    Housing stability account

    Once that happens, some of the money goes into an account to keep the Land Trust alive, obtain resources, and keep the lights on. This account is separate from everything else to ensure housing stability.

  4. Stage 4

    Vacant houses

    Vacant houses will be decorated and furnished for members to stay temporarily for free, or for non-members to stay for donations decided by the surrounding Groups. Once a group picks a house and confirms it is available to buy, it can contact the volunteer Unbought housing team — real estate, legal, and other helpers. Nothing is contacted or transmitted in this prototype.

  5. Stage 5

    Surplus wishlist

    Once the balance sheet is organized and providing a surplus, the surplus goes toward a wishlist. Each member can search multiple items, provide example links, and label each High priority, Medium, or Nice to have. High priority equals 3 points, Medium equals 2, and Nice to have equals 1; the points come from the label, not a typed number. The group for each item is derived from the member's profile. The priority lists are Active working members, Disabled, Over 65, Homeless, Under 25, and Confirmed report within the last year. Each priority list is first come, first served; the waitlist takes the top of each list and cycles through the lists. See the local wishlist draft.

Active checking and surplus

Any leftover budget goes into the surplus fund; money already spoken for goes into the active checking fund. Savings for taxes and utilities count as spoken for.

This prototype holds no money, opens no accounts, and sends no notices. The stages are a plain-language plan for the future, not a promise of housing or a payout.

No-influence policy

A donation is not a vote.

Money can fund the work. It cannot buy the community. These rules apply equally to a $5 gift and a major one.

What donations never buy

  • Governance votes, board seats, or veto power over community decisions.
  • Priority for housing, care, services, or waitlist placement.
  • A say over which listings are published or which neighbors are welcomed.
  • Naming rights, advertising, or a permanent donor class.

What the trust commits to

  • Follow the published priority order and explain any exception in plain language.
  • Publish a ledger of income and spending at least annually, with a prototype-era note until the trust is formed.
  • Keep care unconditional: no one is turned away for being unable to donate.
  • Seek legal and fiscal advice before accepting property, cash at scale, or anything with tax consequences.

Prototype limits

  • There is no payment processor, escrow account, or backend on this page.
  • No card, bank, or tax details are requested, and nothing is transmitted.
  • A saved intent is not a legal pledge, a receipt, or proof of a gift.
  • Before accepting real donations, Unbought will publish its legal and tax status and use a regulated payment provider.
