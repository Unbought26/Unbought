Community standards Guidelines & consequences

The consent commandments.

Unbought is not held together by money or management. It is held together by consent, honesty, and neighbors willing to answer for what they do. These guidelines apply to every member, in every space of the commons.

Prototype boundary. This is a local-first model of community accountability. In this build nothing is reported to a server, no authority is contacted, and no enforcement action is carried out. Cases, reviews, and votes are recorded only in this browser.

The Ten Consent Commandments

  1. Nothing without consent.

    An offer is an invitation, never a claim on anyone. A request needs a clear yes. Silence, hesitation, or a yes given under pressure is not consent. Anyone may change their mind or withdraw at any time, and the answer is accepted without argument.

  2. No money changes hands between neighbors.

    Labor and goods are free. Only exact, receipt-backed material costs may change hands when supplies cannot be found free, and a vehicle recipient pays state registration and titling fees to the state, not to Unbought. No wages, tips, barter scores, payouts, or cryptocurrency.

  3. Tell the truth about what you offer.

    Describe the real condition of goods, the real scope of a skill, and any materials needed. Regulated work must be labeled honestly as general peer education or as licensed clinical or legal service with an active license. Never imply a qualification you do not hold.

  4. Private information stays private.

    A member's legal name, street address, ZIP, housing status, birthdate, and phone are never published. The public resume shows only a chosen first name, picture, tag line, bio, profession, member-selected City/State, skills, commons totals, availability, and a phone number if that member opts in. Do not repeat or infer another person's private details.

  5. Keep every exchange safe.

    No weapons, medication, recalled goods, unsafe items, or gift cards for major corporations. Agree on scope, timing, and access needs before showing up. Respect scent-free rooms, chairs, captions, and boundaries. Safety reports are local, tied to the post or message, and never sent to police or any outside service in this prototype.

  6. No extraction, no leverage.

    Do not use someone's need against them. No guilt, pressure, unpaid obligation, grooming, favor banking, or tracking another member's every move. Mutual aid is not a transaction and care is not a debt.

  7. Every person has worth without work.

    Participation is not a productivity score. A neighbor who cannot work this month is still a neighbor. Tell the truth when capacity changes so the community can respond with care instead of punishment.

  8. Be honest in the prototype.

    There are no real accounts, payments, messages to other people, or uploads. Say plainly when an action is only recorded locally. Do not present a local note, intent, or vote as if it reached someone else or has legal force.

  9. Accept review and answer for harm.

    If you are reported or a guideline is broken, community members review the facts. Attach relevant local evidence when you have it, respond in your own words, and do not retaliate against a reporter, reviewer, or voter. Retaliation is its own violation.

  10. Protect the commons for the next person.

    Invite people in, share skills, and leave the space safer than you found it. When the community decides a limit or a revocation is needed, accept the outcome and use the reflection period to come back right, if and when the community allows it.

Consequences and care, step by step

Nothing here is automatic. Each step begins with a community review of the facts, and the goal is always to understand what happened before deciding what access makes sense.

Monthly pledge review

If you do not fulfill your pledge for the month, your account becomes inactive and you can only observe until the community reviews the situation.

Why weren't you working?

Before any limit is set, community members review why. A recent tragedy, moving, loss of ability to work, illness, caregiving, or another reason may mean you belong in the care circle instead of being treated as noncompliant.

The care circle

In the care circle you can access basic necessities only. If you can prove with evidence that you cannot work, the community may adjust that limit. The evidence stays local in this prototype; nothing is transmitted.

Guideline or law violation

If you violate a community guideline or a law, your account is limited to basic necessities while community members review the case. This is a safety boundary, not a declaration of guilt by the prototype.

Repeated offenses and exile

Repeated offenses can result in “exile” — revocation of membership. Community members review every case, and a revocation requires a major vote of the majority, not a decision by one person.

Reflection and reapplication

If your access is revoked, you enter a reflection period whose length is decided by the community. Only after that period can you apply again. Your application goes immediately to review and a vote; the majority that decides is made up of members within your area.

Who votes on a review board case

To keep the process impartial and connected to the issue, the involved parties, friends of the involved parties, Observers, and Care Circle members do not vote on the case. They are still encouraged to provide evidence and join the conversation in the review board.

Voting is open to pledged members whose physical address is in the same county as an involved party. If one member is in State A and the reported member is in State B, members in both respective counties may vote. County eligibility is based on your physical address, not your mailing address. Members of the same group may also vote unless they have a report against them from the last year. Members of allied groups may vote as well, unless the group voted to close its review board to alliances outside its own ZIP code; alliances within that ZIP code are always included. Working members of groups and alliances vote on alliance terms and guidelines. Counties and street addresses stay private and never appear on a public resume; nothing is transmitted.

Repair, therapy, and proof of treatment

Therapy is encouraged for anyone who has violated a guideline. In some cases the community may require a therapeutic evaluation and proof that recommended treatment was completed or sustained before full access returns. This is about repair and safety, not shame. No diagnosis or therapy record is published on a public resume; in this prototype those notes stay in the member's local record only.

What each standing means

Active

Full participation: post goods and labor, request exchanges, chat, and help review cases.

Inactive · observe only

No posting or requesting until the monthly pledge review is complete.

Care circle

Basic necessities only, with a path to adjusted access when evidence shows work is not possible.

Limited

Basic necessities only while a guideline or law violation is under community review.

Student

Basic necessities only while enrolled in the Academy. Finishing the program and the promised part-time year restores full participation; an unfinished program creates a tuition obligation to the Unbought donation fund.

Exiled

Membership revoked by majority vote, followed by a community-decided reflection period before reapplication.
