Good questions Frequently asked

The honest answers, in plain language.

Unbought is a prototype for a consent-based, currency-free mutual aid society. Nothing here processes payments or sends messages to other people. Public listings and community posts are shared through Supabase; pledges and private details stay in this browser unless you turn on optional private sync in Dashboard → Settings; messages, report text, and evidence stay here; no payment is processed; with optional private sync on, only the reported member's local ID and an open/settled status are sent.

The basics

What is Unbought?

A practice of sharing labor, goods, and care without money changing hands between neighbors. The site is a local-first prototype: public listings and community posts can be shared through Supabase, while pledges and private details stay in this browser unless you turn on optional private sync in Dashboard → Settings; messages, report text, and evidence stay here; no payment is processed; with optional private sync on, only the reported member's local ID and an open/settled status are sent.

What does consent mean here?

Nothing is taken, assigned, or published about you without your agreement. Offers are invitations, requests need a clear yes, and your public profile shows only what you choose to share.

Is this a real service right now?

Yes, as an open beta. It is still local-first and there is no payment processing; optional private sync uses a separate Supabase Auth account. A public-data-only Supabase bridge shares listings, group cards, and community posts; pledges and private details stay in this browser unless you turn on optional private sync in Dashboard → Settings; messages, report text, and evidence stay here; no payment is processed; with optional private sync on, only the reported member's local ID and an open/settled status are sent.

Is Unbought a government?

No. Unbought is a voluntary mutual-aid commons with self-governance features. Members help shape the rules, review disputes, and share care. It does not claim territory, taxes, police power, or authority over anyone who does not consent. If it ever became a legally recognized public body, that would be a separate formal process; size alone does not make it a government.

Is this a cult?

There is no single official definition, so the fair way to answer is to look at behavior. The main warning sign of a cult is high control: pressure to join or stay, isolation from family or outside information, one unquestionable leader, strict identity rules, forced labor or money, fear or shame, and penalties for leaving.

Unbought is built to work the other way. Membership is voluntary, browsing is open to everyone, and someone can withdraw from the pledge without losing their property or being punished. Members set their own boundaries, keep private details on their own device unless they opt in to sync, and take part through peer review rather than a single ruler. There are no dues, no taxes, no paid leaders, no housing tied to obedience, and no claim to control anyone outside the group.

Some parts may feel unfamiliar — the pledge, shared access rules, Care Circle decisions, review boards, and language about a commons — and any large group can drift toward control if it stops protecting consent. If Unbought ever pressured people to stay, hid information, punished disagreement, demanded money or labor under threat, or placed one person above review, that would be a serious problem worth naming.

As it is built now, the honest label is a voluntary mutual-aid commons, not a cult.

How does site search work?

The search in the header is on every page. It looks through page copy, Free Labor and Free Stuff listings, current members, and saved groups in this browser. Results are local links; the search itself sends nothing.

Is the beta open outside the Poconos?

Yes. The soft launch is open to anyone, anywhere; we are not limiting beta membership to one region. The beta has fewer features while we finish building the prototype. Some location examples still use Pocono-area towns such as Stroudsburg, East Stroudsburg, Mount Pocono, and Jim Thorpe, but they are only examples. You can enter your own location.

Membership and privacy

What does taking the pledge do?

It records your membership locally, along with the legal name and contact details the prototype needs to model real intake. It is not a legal contract. Those details are sent only if you explicitly turn on optional private sync in Dashboard → Settings.

Can I choose whether my private details are stored online?

Yes. Private sync is off by default. Dashboard → Settings has a “Sync my private data” section. Turning it on requires a separate Supabase Auth email and password plus an explicit consent checkbox. Only then are your member ID, account details, pledge details, and waiting-list entry uploaded, where only you and the current rotating web-developer admins can read them. If you have also recorded the Web development volunteer offer, only your member ID is added to the automatic admin pool; no note or other role detail is uploaded, and no admins are active until more than seven web developers have opted in. Filing or settling a report also sends only the reported member's local ID and an open/settled status; report text, evidence, reasons, and reporter identity never leave this browser. Passwords are handled by Supabase Auth; nothing from local sign-in is uploaded. After you turn sync on once, uploads happen automatically. You can check sync, turn sync off, or delete your cloud copy at any time. Public listing and community-post sync is separate and unchanged.

What does my public profile show?

Your chosen first name, locally stored picture, tag line, bio, profession, the City/State you select, your skills, confirmed hours worked, items gifted, other commons totals, your availability, and a public phone number only if you opt in. It may show a pending or live seed/founder tag. It never shows your cell phone, legal name, street or mailing addresses, ZIP, housing status, birthdate, group, report count, comments, interactions, or member ID. Signed-in members can switch between the public resume and the private profile with View as, choose separate profile colors, and add friends from another member’s public profile.

Who can enter my work hours?

No one can add hours unilaterally. The worker and the receiver each choose the same agreed time from fixed options. Matching confirmations log that time; nothing is typed into an hours counter. If the choices differ, evidence goes to a review board and three randomly drawn active members choose one of the confirmed times; care circle members, students, observers, and either party cannot serve. Only those confirmed hours appear in your week, month, year, and all-time totals. After probation, a full week with no confirmed work freezes the pledge while the monthly hours are still short; confirmed hours reset that week clock. A frozen pledge blocks service requests until the pledged hours are completed. More than 30 days frozen makes you an Observer, and a re-pledge is accepted only after three impartial active members approve it.

Why ask for a legal name and addresses at all?

The intake mirrors what a real mutual-aid trust would need for accountability and emergency contact. In this prototype it stays in your browser and is never shown on your public resume.

Can people under 18 join?

Full membership still opens at 18; the sign-up form blocks birthdates that would make someone under 18. Young-neighbor learning paths are future plans on What’s to Come, not part of this working build.

What can each membership track access?

Active Contributors take part in the full commons. Apprentices may use Basic Necessities only while enrolled. Care Circle members start with Basic Necessities and no labor obligation; full access can be confirmed locally without storing proof details. A frozen pledge cannot request services or change shared content until the pledged hours are completed, but the member can still offer labor and read the documents. After more than 30 days frozen the member becomes an Observer and can read or explore only until three active members approve a re-pledge. These boundaries are part of the Community Guidelines and are enforced locally in this prototype.

What are Basic Necessities, and who decides how mutual aid works?

Basic Necessities are Food, Health Care, Dental, Mental Health, Shelter, Clothing, Gas Cards, and Uber Gift cards. No luxury items or nice to haves. Groups can decide how mutual aid will work within these limits.

What is an alliance?

Alliances are other groups within the Unbought network. Groups decide whether to accept an alliance between them, and working members vote on alliance terms and guidelines.

What do the Seed, Beta Founder, and first-year badges mean?

A seed member is the first person of a profession to sign up in a ZIP code and gets a badge such as Dancer Seed, Law Seed, Doctor Seed, or Cleaning Seed. Everyone who signs from the database build until rollout gets a Beta Founder badge. In the first official year, non-seeds get a 1st Year badge and seeds get a First year Founder badge. Membership years begin at the exact moment of the vernal equinox, not on January 1. The beta build period runs from the March 20, 2026 equinox at 14:46 UTC to the March 20, 2027 equinox at 20:25 UTC — 4:25pm Eastern.

When do member labels appear?

The rollout target is the exact 2027 vernal equinox, March 20 at 4:25pm Eastern. Until then the dashboard shows Seed and Beta Founder labels as pending so nobody is presented as a first-year member before the rollout moment. Volunteer badges are active locally as soon as an offer is recorded.

Is there a celebration planned?

Gatherings and every other future feature are listed only on What’s to Come. Nothing is booked or promised in this working build.

Do seed members get a thank-you message?

No email is sent in this prototype. Future outreach plans are listed only on What’s to Come.

Goods, labor, and exchanges

What can I offer on Free Stuff?

Legal, safe goods: groceries, household items, clothing, furniture, tech, tools, solar panels, raw materials, vehicles, gift cards from independent small businesses and co-ops, and other useful things. Vehicles are free; the recipient pays state registration and titling fees. Major-corporation gift cards, weapons, medication, recalled goods, and unsafe items are not allowed.

What can I offer on Free Labor?

Any safe skill you can freely share: repair, care, tutoring, organizing, design, and more. If materials are needed, say so. Regulated work requires an honesty check about licensing. The labor is never billed; any materials are agreed on separately at exact cost.

What if my job title is not listed?

Choose “Write my own title” when posting. It is saved locally in Dashboard → Suggestions with the title, pillar, listing, and location; nothing is transmitted.

Can I add photos?

Yes. Members can attach up to three photos to a goods or labor post. They are resized in your browser and saved locally. They are never uploaded.

How do I find something nearby?

Free Labor can be searched by title, skill, or neighbor, then filtered by pillar, that pillar’s service titles, online or in-person format, city, state, and ZIP prefix. Free Stuff filters by category, condition, color, brand, city, state, and ZIP prefix. Both directories can be sorted by newest, title, location, or nearest to a ZIP you enter, and active filters can be removed one at a time.

Can non-members get anything?

Anyone can browse the directories. Mutual-aid requests and posting are for pledged members. Labor and goods shared here are free through mutual consent, never a purchase.

What totals can members see?

Members can see their own confirmed hours and item counts by week, month, year, and all time. No real money is held, moved, or paid out. Hours and item counts appear only after mutual confirmation or review. Your legal name, addresses, ZIP, birthdate, group, and report count stay out of the public resume.

Safety and accountability

How do safety reports work?

Reports are tied to the thing you were looking at — a listing, a comment on a listing, a message, community or social chat, a profile, or a dashboard item — so you never have to type someone’s name into a report form. You can attach local messages, pictures, or other files as evidence. Report text, evidence, reasons, and reporter identity always stay in this browser. With optional private sync on, the only report data sent is the reported member's local ID plus an open/settled status so private-data access for the reported web developer can pause.

How do I acknowledge the labor and product safety standards?

Members read and acknowledge the Labor & Product Safety Standards when they sign the pledge. Posting, editing, and requesting also ask for an acknowledgment if the standards version has changed. The record is stored on this device with the date, your local member ID, and the standards version. You can review it and download a copy in Dashboard → Settings. It is not sent to Supabase. It records that you read the rules; it is not legal advice or a promise that an exchange is risk-free.

Who is allowed to vote in a review board case?

Involved parties, friends of involved parties, Observers, and Care Circle members do not vote, so the decision stays impartial. They can still attach evidence and take part in the review board conversation. Voting is open to pledged members whose physical address is in the same county as an involved party. If one member is in State A and the reported member is in State B, members in both respective counties may vote. Members of the same group may also vote unless they have a report against them from the last year, and members of allied groups may vote unless the group voted to close its review board to alliances outside its own ZIP code; alliances within that ZIP code are always included. Working members vote on alliance terms and guidelines. County eligibility uses your physical address; counties and street addresses stay private and never appear on a public resume.

Why are there no police reports?

This prototype does not contact authorities or any outside service. The review board is a local, consent-based model for thinking about accountability, not a legal process.

What happens during a reflection period?

Some full-access actions pause while a member works through a locally tracked reflection and repair status in their dashboard. No therapy notes, diagnoses, medical records, or treatment details are saved; only the resulting access status and time may be kept. There is no outside review or reporting.

Will friends and groups be part of this?

Yes. Dashboard → Friends & Groups automatically places you in the main town group, such as Stroudsburg Group I. When a town group reaches 150 people, another Stroudsburg group opens with a Roman numeral — II, III, … XI — and neighbors can choose which numbered group to join. The first member of a new numbered group is not a seed member. The town group profile shows the city, state, and ZIP below its name. Every other group is a social or support circle you make or join, and group names link to their own group profile.

The seven local group admin seats rotate weekly for workers only, staggered across one-to-seven-week cycles; these are separate from the rotating web-developer admins who can read optional Supabase private data. Care Circle, Observer, and Apprentice members cannot hold local group admin seats. When notified, an admin must read the Unbought Community Guidelines and the group’s guidelines and accept by 6pm the day before the term starts. If they decline or miss that time, a backup admin is chosen. Active admins can add another admin, cancel a group, delete group chat posts and replies, require an impartial review, change the group’s community guidelines, and start a guideline vote in chat. You can leave a non-town group when another active admin exists; only an active admin can cancel a group. Reports made by a temporary admin need evidence, go to an impartial review, and are never decided as criminal matters by member vote. Admin votes on guideline changes are recorded as Agreed, Not agreed, or More discussion needed.

Search current members by chosen name, profession, or town and send a local friend request; public profiles have the same Add friend action. You can keep one group of your own and write social chat drafts with exactly Audience, Title, Message, and picture. There is one local account, so no invitations are sent and no one else’s account changes. Friend lists and groups are capped at 150 people.

Can a group create a business or a brand?

Group businesses and brands are future plans listed only on What’s to Come. The controls stay hidden in this Phase 1 build, and nothing is transmitted.

Current groups can set their physical location or mark themselves Online in Group settings. Any co-op legal information is only a local note; forming a co-op is a legal step outside Unbought, and nothing is filed or transmitted here. Every group setting stays in this browser.

What do dashboard notifications show?

Local drafts — messages, requests, comments, group posts, social chat drafts, and a one-time notice when a review board case opens and you are eligible to vote. If you are involved, a friend of someone involved, an Observer, or in the Care Circle, you will not get a vote notice, but you can still add evidence and join the conversation. Each notice is one whole link: clicking anywhere on the item marks it read and opens the target. Listing comments stay with their post on Free Labor or Free Stuff. Nothing is delivered to another person or device from this prototype.

Can I talk with neighbors on a listing?

Yes. Each Free Labor and Free Stuff post has a comment thread with replies, and every comment has its own report button. The whole card is a link to the details dialog, and an opened card can be restored with a ?listing= local link. Group profiles are separate: they have Members, Admins, Group chat, Offers in Free Stuff & Free Labor, and Group details. Group chat replies stay attached to the post they answer; there are no separate group comments or Group involvement block, and listing comments are not copied into a group.

Where are the community guidelines and consequences?

They are on the Community Guidelines page. They include the Ten Consent Commandments, monthly pledge review, the care circle, access limits, repair and reflection, community review, majority votes, exile, and the reflection period before reapplication.

Prototype details

Where is my data stored?

In your browser’s local storage under the Unbought keys. Clearing site data or using the Clear this browser's demo data control removes it. There is no account recovery because there is no server account.

Does the site track me or show ads?

No. There are no analytics, ads, tracking scripts, or external frameworks. The only third-party embed is the privacy-enhanced Alan Watts video on the pledge page. The single catalog-style layout on Free Stuff is a visual nod only, built with Unbought’s own content and colors.

How do direct member messages work?

Direct messages open in your own mail app; the site sends nothing by itself. Create a separate email just for Unbought and publish that address in Dashboard → Settings if you want members to contact you directly. Do not publish your private, work, or school email. Published addresses are public by design. If a member has not published a contact address, your message opens as a draft to unbought26@gmail.com for a one-time introduction. Local folders and stars stay on this device. Trash holds local message records until you restore them or choose Delete forever or Empty Trash.

How do beta emails work?

Automatic email needs a paid service later. For this free beta, Dashboard → Settings has pre-filled welcome, Care Circle, posting, request, review board, and group welcome messages. They open in your own mail app and nothing is sent until you press Send. The site does not store an email log or send messages in the background.

How do I report a bug or suggest something?

Use the suggestion form on the dashboard, or the waiting-list form on What’s to Come. Both record locally in this prototype.
